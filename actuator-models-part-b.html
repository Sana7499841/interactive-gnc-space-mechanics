4.4 Part B — Control Authority, Constraints, Uncertainty, and Verification

Part A established how individual actuators generate force, torque, momentum exchange, and impulse.

Part B now moves from the physics of an individual actuator to the capability of the complete spacecraft actuation system.

The main questions are

\[ \boxed{ \text{What force and torque can the spacecraft actually generate?} } \]

\[ \boxed{ \text{Which control directions are strong, weak, or unavailable?} } \]

\[ \boxed{ \text{What physical constraints reduce the available authority?} } \]

\[ \boxed{ \text{How do uncertainty, degradation, and failure affect actuation?} } \]

\[ \boxed{ \text{How is the complete actuator model verified?} }. \]

The progression is

\[ \boxed{\text{Control Authority}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Constraints}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Uncertainty / Failure}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Actuator Models}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Verification}}. \]

Each topic below opens a dedicated page where the mathematics, engineering interpretation, examples, simulation methods, and spacecraft applications can be developed in detail.

From Control Authority to Control Allocation

Section 4.4 determines what the installed actuator system can physically produce.

Once a desired spacecraft wrench has been shown to be feasible, the next question is

\[ \boxed{ \text{Which individual actuators should generate that wrench?} }. \]

The transition is

\[ \boxed{\text{Desired Wrench}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Control Allocation}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Individual Actuator Commands}} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{Realized Wrench}}. \]

Detailed allocation methods such as pseudoinverse allocation, weighted least squares, constrained optimization, thruster selection, pulse-width modulation, and fault-aware allocation belong in the later section on Control Allocation and Actuator Commanding.
