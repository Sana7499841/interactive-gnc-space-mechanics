Part A · Mission definition

Why staging is a hybrid-dynamics problem

A two-stage launch vehicle climbs through a simplified vertical atmosphere. Stage 1 consumes propellant, reaches burnout, releases its dry structure, coasts briefly, and starts Stage 2 with a finite ignition ramp. The vehicle does not teleport or gain velocity merely because mass is discarded.

1 · Powered Stage 1

Thrust and mass flow act together; the vehicle becomes lighter as fuel is burned.

2 · Burnout and separation

Thrust shuts off; a discrete dry-mass jettison changes the mass state, not the altitude or velocity.

3 · Coast and Stage 2

During delay, gravity and drag continue. Stage 2 then ramps to commanded thrust.

Model scope: Educational 1D vertical ascent with no guidance steering, gravity turn, rotation, separation impulse, throttle feedback, or engine shutdown tail-off. These effects matter in flight-quality 6-DOF simulations.
Part B · Governing equations

Continuous dynamics and discrete resets

State vector \(x=[h,v,m]^T\), with altitude \(h\), upward velocity \(v\), and instantaneous total vehicle mass \(m\).

\[\dot h=v,\quad \dot v=\frac{T-\frac12\rho(h)C_D A v|v|}{m}-g(h),\quad \dot m=-\frac{T}{I_{sp}g_0}\]
\[\rho(h)=\rho_0e^{-h/H},\qquad g(h)=g_0\left(\frac{R_E}{R_E+h}\right)^2\]

At the stage-separation instant \(t_s\), impose the state reset:

\[h^+=h^-,\qquad v^+=v^-,\qquad m^+=m^- - m_{dry,1}\]

Stage 2 ignition follows \(t_i=t_s+\Delta t_{coast}\). Its thrust ramps from zero to \(T_2\) over \(\tau_r\), while the propellant mass flow follows actual thrust.

\[T(t)=T_2\,\min\left(1,\max\left(0,\frac{t-t_i}{\tau_r}\right)\right)\]

For zero ramp duration, ignition is modeled as an instantaneous step. The ideal mass reset is a mathematical event, not a continuous high-rate mass-flow pulse.

Part G · Reproducible engineering implementation

Python and MATLAB starting points

import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp

g0, Re, rho0, H = 9.80665, 6371000., 1.225, 8500.

def rhs(t, x, thrust, isp, Cd=0.4, A=8.):
    h, v, m = x
    rho = rho0*np.exp(-max(h, 0)/H)
    drag = 0.5*rho*Cd*A*v*abs(v)
    grav = g0*(Re/(Re+max(h, 0)))**2
    return [v, (thrust-drag)/m-grav,
            -thrust/(isp*g0) if thrust > 0 else 0.]

# Integrate each phase separately, with exact event boundaries.
# Example: first-stage burn
T1, Isp1, prop1 = 2e6, 280., 75000.
tb1 = prop1*Isp1*g0/T1
sol1 = solve_ivp(lambda t,x: rhs(t,x,T1,Isp1),
                 (0,tb1), [0.,0.,120000.],
                 rtol=1e-9, atol=1e-9, dense_output=True)

# Explicit separation reset: altitude and velocity unchanged.
x_sep = sol1.y[:,-1].copy()
x_sep[2] -= 15000.

# Coast and Stage 2 are additional integrations with
# thrust=0 and time-dependent ignition-ramp thrust.
# Check mass budget and join segments without smoothing the jump.
g0 = 9.80665; Re = 6371000; rho0 = 1.225; H = 8500;
T1 = 2e6; Isp1 = 280; prop1 = 75000;
tBurn = prop1*Isp1*g0/T1;
x0 = [0; 0; 120000];  % altitude, velocity, mass
rhs = @(t,x,T,Isp) [x(2); ...
    (T - 0.5*rho0*exp(-max(x(1),0)/H)*0.4*8*x(2)*abs(x(2)))/x(3) ...
    - g0*(Re/(Re+max(x(1),0)))^2; ...
    -T/(Isp*g0)];
opts = odeset('RelTol',1e-9,'AbsTol',1e-9);
[t1,x1] = ode45(@(t,x)rhs(t,x,T1,Isp1),[0 tBurn],x0,opts);
xSep = x1(end,:)'; xSep(3) = xSep(3) - 15000;
% Next: integrate zero-thrust coast; then stage-2 ignition ramp.
% Preserve h and v through the separation event.

These snippets demonstrate phase integration and separation; the browser experiment additionally handles Stage 2 depletion and variable ramp thrust. They are starting points rather than full flight dynamics software.

Part H · Engineering takeaway

A smooth trajectory does not prove correct staging logic

Staging is a state-machine problem as much as it is a differential-equation problem. A physically defensible simulation must combine continuous mass depletion, unpowered coast, explicit structural-mass resets, and well-defined ignition events. Verify event continuity and mass conservation before interpreting acceleration spikes or tuning a control system.
