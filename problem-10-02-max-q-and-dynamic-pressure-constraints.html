Part A · Mission scenario

Why a rocket encounters Max-Q

A launch vehicle accelerates as it climbs, while atmospheric density decreases with altitude. Dynamic pressure initially rises because speed increases, then falls because the atmosphere becomes thin. The maximum value, Max-Q, is an important aerodynamic loading indicator.

Model scope: Educational 1D vertical ascent with exponential atmosphere, constant drag coefficient and reference area, variable propellant mass, inverse-square gravity and an idealized throttle controller. No winds, staging, pitch-over, bending moments, lift or structural dynamics. The throttle algorithm is a demonstration, not flight-qualified guidance.
Part B · Mathematical formulation

Dynamic pressure and load equations

\[q=\frac12\rho(h)v_{\mathrm{rel}}^2,\qquad \rho(h)=\rho_0e^{-h/H}\]
\[D=qC_DA,\qquad \dot h=v,\qquad \dot v=\frac{T-D}{m}-\frac{\mu}{(R_E+h)^2},\qquad \dot m=-\frac{T}{I_{sp}g_0}\]

For vertical flight through a stationary atmosphere, differentiating q yields

\[\frac{1}{q}\frac{dq}{dt}=2\frac{\dot v}{v}-\frac{\dot h}{H}\]

At an interior maximum with nonzero velocity, 2a/v = v/H. This is a diagnostic condition, not a separate guidance law. Proper axial acceleration is approximately (T − D)/(mg₀); the aerodynamic drag deceleration is D/m.

Important distinction: q is not a force or structural stress. Aerodynamic force scales as qCDA; bending loads depend on shape, angle of attack and distributed pressures. A Max-Q limit is therefore a simplified mission constraint.

Part G · Python and MATLAB reference

Reproduce the pressure calculation

These short examples calculate dynamic pressure for a prescribed vertical trajectory. The browser simulation additionally integrates the vehicle equations and mass depletion.

import numpy as np
h = np.linspace(0, 80000, 1001)   # m
v = 1200 * (1 - np.exp(-h / 18000))  # illustrative profile
rho = 1.225 * np.exp(-h / 8500)
q = 0.5 * rho * v**2
k = np.argmax(q)
print(f"Max-Q: {q[k]/1000:.2f} kPa at {h[k]/1000:.2f} km")
h = linspace(0,80000,1001); % m
v = 1200*(1-exp(-h/18000)); % illustrative profile
rho = 1.225*exp(-h/8500);
q = 0.5*rho.*v.^2;
[qmax,k] = max(q);
fprintf('Max-Q: %.2f kPa at %.2f km\n',qmax/1000,h(k)/1000);

The illustrative speed profiles above are not solutions of the powered ascent equations. For cross-validation, implement the coupled differential equations using SciPy solve_ivp or MATLAB ode45, including burnout events.

Part H · Verification and engineering judgment

Max-Q is a constraint, not the mission objective

Confirm units (Pa versus kPa), inspect the full q(t) curve rather than a single sample, repeat the integration at half the timestep, and compare with a second numerical solver. A throttle strategy that reduces Max-Q may extend burn time or change the altitude and speed reached at burnout.

Engineering conclusion: The best ascent control must satisfy aerodynamic loading limits while maintaining adequate acceleration, controllability and mission performance. Max-Q alone does not certify structural safety or orbital insertion.
