Problem 10.03 — Thrust Vector Control and Pitch Stability

From gimbal torque and rigid-body pitch dynamics to closed-loop tracking, actuator limitations, aerodynamic instability, uncertainty and numerical validation.

A controller must stabilize the vehicle, not just generate a large moment.
Mission scenario

Why thrust vector control matters

A single-engine launch vehicle climbs through the atmosphere. Its engine gimbals to steer the thrust line relative to the instantaneous centre of mass (CG). A pitch disturbance, aerodynamic moment, actuator delay or CG shift can cause pointing error or unstable oscillation.

Mission objective: Track a prescribed pitch reference, reject disturbances, respect gimbal angle and slew-rate limits, and maintain stability over changing flight conditions.

This educational model considers planar pitch motion. Positive pitch angle \(\theta\), pitch rate \(\omega\), gimbal angle \(\delta\) and TVC torque share a chosen positive convention. In a real rocket the sign depends on engine location and axis definitions.

Model boundaries: rigid body, one effective engine, no bending/slosh, no full 6-DOF trajectory coupling, no lateral translation. Aerodynamic stability is represented by a linearized pitching moment. The simulation is for conceptual design and validation exercises, not flight qualification.

Part A

Gimbal geometry and pitching moment

For a thrust force \(T\) with perpendicular moment arm \(l\) at zero gimbal, \(M_{TVC}=Tl\sin\delta\). For small \(\delta\) in radians, \(M_{TVC}\approx Tl\delta\). The torque-to-angular-acceleration relationship is \(\dot\omega=M/I_{yy}\).

Part C

Closed-loop PD attitude control

Reproducible code

Python and MATLAB reference implementations

These minimal independent reference examples reproduce the simplified constant-inertia, linearized PD pitch dynamics. They intentionally omit actuator lag and aerodynamic terms, which must be added for full comparison with the webpage's nonlinear model.

import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
I, T, l = 80000., 1e6, 4.
Kp, Kd = 160000., 160000.
limit = np.deg2rad(6)
def rhs(t, y):
    theta, omega = y
    desired_torque = -Kp*theta-Kd*omega
    delta = np.clip(desired_torque/(T*l), -limit, limit)
    return [omega, T*l*np.sin(delta)/I]
t = np.linspace(0,18,1801)
sol = solve_ivp(rhs,(0,18),[np.deg2rad(5),0],t_eval=t,rtol=1e-9,atol=1e-11)
plt.plot(sol.t,np.rad2deg(sol.y[0]))
plt.xlabel('Time [s]');plt.ylabel('Pitch [deg]');plt.grid();plt.show()
I=80000; T=1e6; l=4; Kp=160000; Kd=160000;
lim=deg2rad(6);
f=@(t,y) [y(2); T*l*sin(max(-lim,min(lim,(-Kp*y(1)-Kd*y(2))/(T*l))))/I];
opts=odeset('RelTol',1e-9,'AbsTol',1e-11);
[t,y]=ode45(f,[0 18],[deg2rad(5);0],opts);
plot(t,rad2deg(y(:,1)),'LineWidth',1.8);grid on;
xlabel('Time [s]');ylabel('Pitch [deg]');
title('Pitch response with gimbal saturation');
Engineering takeaway

What a launch-vehicle GNC engineer should conclude

Control authority

Thrust and lever arm determine how much pitching moment a gimbal can deliver.

Closed-loop stability

Controller gains must be assessed together with inertia, aerodynamic derivatives, actuator bandwidth and limits.

Flight relevance

Rigid-body pitch control is only the beginning; bending, slosh, sensor noise, guidance and six-degree-of-freedom coupling matter in actual launch vehicles.

Final insight: More aggressive gains are not always better. Robust TVC design balances tracking, damping, control authority and uncertainty.
